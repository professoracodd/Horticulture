Part 1: Hormones and Tropisms
Scenario

A grower notices that a tomato seedling on a windowsill is bending noticeably toward the window. Meanwhile, a cutting placed horizontally in a propagation tray sends its root tip growing downward and its stem tip growing upward within a few days.

1.Name the two tropisms described in this scenario and explain what each one is a response to.

Self-check only, not a grade

2.Which hormone is primarily responsible for the bending and directional growth in this scenario? Explain how it produces opposite effects in shoots versus roots.

Self-check only, not a grade

3.The grower decides to prune (pinch) the top of a second seedling. Predict what will happen to the axillary buds below the cut and explain why, using the term apical dominance.

Self-check only, not a grade
Part 2: Stages of Growth
Scenario

A student plants bean seeds in a germination chamber. Over the next two weeks, they record the following observations, but not in order: (a) two sets of true leaves appear, (b) the seed coat cracks, (c) the radicle emerges, (d) cotyledons emerge above the soil, (e) the hypocotyl breaks the soil surface, (f) the cotyledons wither and fall off.

4.Put observations (a) through (f) in the correct order and name which stage of growth (germination or seedling) each one belongs to.

Self-check only, not a grade

5.One seedling in the chamber grows unusually tall and thin compared to the others. What environmental factor most likely explains this, and what does that tell you about the plant's growth response?

Self-check only, not a grade

6.Define meristem, and explain the difference between what a shoot apical meristem produces and what a lateral meristem produces.

Self-check only, not a grade

7.The student's beans are dicots. Based on what you know about seed structure, would you expect their beans to be albuminous or exalbuminous? Explain your reasoning.

Self-check only, not a grade
