What Are Blue Zones?

Blue Zones are five geographic regions where people live measurably longer, healthier lives — with the highest concentrations of centenarians on Earth. Research published in Ageing Research Reviews (Scapagnini et al., University of Molise) suggests their longevity is driven less by genetics and more by polyphenol-rich diets that modulate the core biological hallmarks of aging: genomic instability, telomere attrition, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, and chronic inflammation.
🇯🇵

Okinawa, Japan

Highest prevalence of centenarians — lower cardiovascular disease, dementia & diabetes

▼

Low-calorie, nutrient-dense, antioxidant-rich diet. Centenarians show healthier blood chemistry profiles than average populations.

Key Foods
Purple Sweet Potato (Beni Imo)Tofu / MisoTurmericGreen TeaLeafy Greens
Key Polyphenols
Anthocyanins (sweet potato)Isoflavones / FOXO3 (soy)Curcumin (turmeric)EGCG (green tea)

Anthocyanins combat genomic instability and stem cell exhaustion. Soy isoflavones activate the longevity protein FOXO3. Curcumin reduces mitochondrial dysfunction. EGCG fights telomere shortening and supports autophagy.

🇮🇹

Sardinia, Italy

Highest concentration of male centenarians in the world

▼

Genetics not linked to longevity here — diet and lifestyle are the primary drivers. Coffee's chlorogenic acid is now considered the key polyphenol source, not red wine (whose consumption was actually lower in the Blue Zone area).

Key Foods
Fruits & VegetablesGoat DairyLegumesWhole GrainsCoffee
Key Polyphenols
Chlorogenic Acid (coffee)

Chlorogenic acid improves vascular function, reduces inflammation, and targets epigenetic alterations — a hallmark of aging linked to cancer risk.

🇬🇷

Ikaria, Greece

~30% of population lives past age 90

▼

62–69% of elderly Ikarians adhere to a Mediterranean diet. Long-term adherence inversely correlated with multiple age-related diseases.

Key Foods
Olive OilChickpeas & LentilsLettuce & OnionsFresh Fruit
Key Polyphenols
Oleuropein (olive oil)

Oleuropein is antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective. Targets senescent cells and stem cell exhaustion. Daily olive oil linked to lower all-cause mortality.

🇺🇸

Loma Linda, California

Life expectancy ~10 years longer than rest of California

▼

Seventh-day Adventist community — high prevalence of vegetarians, no alcohol or tobacco. Vegetarian diet associated with reduced all-cause mortality. Note: B12 supplementation is essential without animal products.

Key Foods
Chickpeas & BeansSoy ProductsNutsWhole Grains
Key Polyphenols
Isoflavones (soy & legumes)Flavonoids (fruits & vegetables)
🇨🇷

Nicoya Peninsula, Costa Rica

~29% lower mortality for those 60+ vs. rest of Costa Rica

▼

Lower blood sugar and triglycerides than average. Centenarians eat fruit 1–3 times daily, preferring mangoes. Staple diet: rice, black beans, squash. Mineral-rich volcanic water may also contribute.

Key Foods
Mango & PapayaBlack BeansRice & SquashMeat & Dairy (moderate)
Key Polyphenols
Mangiferin (mango)Flavonoids (beans)

Mangiferin has anti-inflammatory effects against cardiovascular disease, cancer, and neurodegenerative diseases — targeting chronic inflammation, a core hallmark of aging.

The Common Thread: Polyphenol-Rich Diets

Across all five Blue Zones, diets are rich in fruits, vegetables, and legumes containing diverse polyphenols that modulate the hallmarks of aging. While genetics plays a debatable role, the evidence points strongly to diet as the primary environmental driver of exceptional longevity.

Source: Scapagnini et al., Ageing Research Reviews — University of Molise, Italy
